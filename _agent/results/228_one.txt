   5 [heading ] Abstract
   6 [body    ] We introduce SEA-HELM Audio, a Southeast Asian (SEA) multilingual evaluation framework that extends the text-based SEA-HELM benchmark to assess performance degradation in cascaded automatic speech recognition and large language model (ASR–LLM) pipelines.
   7 [body    ] Using synthesized speech, we compare speech-input and text-input performance across five languages—Chinese, English, Malay, Indonesian, and Tamil—on multiple-choice reasoning and instruction-following tasks.
   8 [body    ] We evaluate open-weight and commercial ASR–LLM systems using identical verifiers and quantify the speech gap as the difference between text and speech accuracy.
   9 [body    ] Results show substantial degradation, with accuracy drops of 9.00–18.50 percentage points for multiple-choice reasoning and 20.00–42.86 points for instruction following.
  10 [body    ] Tamil exhibits the largest degradation, exceeding 40 percentage points.
  11 [body    ] Our findings highlight the limitations of text-only benchmarks in predicting voice-agent reliability and demonstrate the value of SEA- HELM Audio for evaluating multilingual voice-agent systems.

  13 [heading ] 1. Introduction
  14 [body    ] As artificial intelligence systems transition from text-based interfaces to conversational voice-enabled agents, evaluating the end-to-end accuracy of cascaded speech-language stacks has become a critical research priority.
  15 [body    ] While text-based Large Language Models (LLMs) continue to achieve high benchmark scores, deploying these models within voice pipelines introduces acoustic transcription errors, phonetic ambiguities, and domain-specific degradation.
  16 [body    ] Quantifying this degradation, specifically measuring the accuracy penalty a user incurs by speaking rather than typing, is essential for understanding realworld agent reliability across diverse tasks.
  17 [body    ] Existing spoken language model benchmarks, such as VoiceBench [1], evaluate capabilities including speech recognition, reasoning, and instruction following through spoken inputs.
  18 [body    ] However, their predominantly English-language evaluation settings provide limited insight into how voice-agent systems perform on Southeast Asian languages and tasks.
  19 [body    ] In particular, it remains unclear to what extent transcription errors alter task-critical information and how these errors affect downstream task performance, especially in the context of Southeast Asian languages.
